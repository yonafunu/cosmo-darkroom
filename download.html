First launch · macOS
The application is not signed with an Apple Developer account. The first time, right-click it in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. On recent macOS: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway.
First launch · Windows
The installer is not signed. If SmartScreen shows “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. If the installer stops with “error 5” or asks for administrator rights, use the portable version: unzip it into a folder of your choice and run COSMO.exe.
First launch · Linux
Make the file executable (chmod +x COSMO-*.AppImage, or Properties › Allow executing), then open it. If nothing happens, install FUSE 2 (sudo apt install libfuse2 on Ubuntu) or take the archive: unpack it and run COSMO/COSMO.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Is the software free?

Yes: COSMO, its stacker and its tools, with no account and no subscription. The RC-Astro engines are an optional complement, subject to their publisher's licence.

What equipment does it work with?

COSMO processes any deep-sky image in FITS, XISF or TIFF. The Stack tool reads the raw subs of a colour sensor, dual-band files (including those of the Dwarf Draco, with their HO and SO layers) and the subs of a monochrome camera with a filter wheel (L, R, G, B, Hα, OIII, SII). The images on this site were taken with a Seestar S50 Pro.

Do I need GraXpert, StarNet or the RC-Astro tools?

No. COSMO carries its three networks, COSMOSharp, COSMODenoise and COSMOStars, which are experimental. GraXpert, StarNet and the RC-Astro tools are used as engines when they are installed on the computer.

Which formats are read and exported?

Read: FITS, XISF, TIFF, PNG, JPEG. Export: 32-bit FITS, 16-bit TIFF, PNG, JPEG; the last three carry the sRGB profile.

Do the measurements have scientific value?

FWHM, limiting magnitude and signal-to-noise ratio serve to assess a stack. Magnitudes are referred to the Gaia G band: they are estimates, not calibrated photometry.

Design

What the software guarantees.

Traceability

Every process reports what it measured: number of stars used, subs left out and why, gains applied, motion of the comet. A setting with no effect says why.

Published methods

Generalised hyperbolic stretch, à trous wavelet transform, photometric calibration on a catalogue, HDR composition in linear light. Implementations written from the published equations.

Optional external engines

GraXpert (sky background), StarNet (stars) and the RC-Astro engines (sharpening, noise, stars) are used from the user's own installation, when present and licensed. The other tools do not depend on them.

Every step under control

Each step is set, previewed, then applied, and stays adjustable afterwards. One click applies the remaining steps of a pipeline with their settings and stops at a step that needs you.
